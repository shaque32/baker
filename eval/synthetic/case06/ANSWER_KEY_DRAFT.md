Reading rules (how the key reads approximate or defined wording):

1. Times are the phones' own clock, America/Los_Angeles (paragraph 8), never a printed value. Item 1 prints UTC+0. Item 2 prints each row's own offset. Item 3 prints every row at UTC-8, so from 3 a.m. on March 9 (when the clocks went forward) its printed times are an hour behind the phone.
2. Windows follow the conventions: 'at about H:MM' is 8 minutes before to 8 minutes after, end exclusive; 'at H:MM' is that minute; 'between H1 and H2' runs from H1:00 through the whole H2:00 minute; a date is midnight to the next midnight; a date range runs from midnight on its first day to the midnight after its last; a day set by an event is that whole day.
3. 'About four minutes' is 180 to 300 s, 'about two minutes' 90 to 150 s, 'about a minute' 45 to 75 s, 'about ten minutes' 540 to 660 s.
4. Windows no convention covers (each listed in SIGNING_SHEET.md): 'within an hour after' the KITTIWAKE THEFT (about 11:15 p.m., March 13) is 11:15 p.m. to 12:15 a.m.; 'within half an hour after' the RECOVERY (10:40 a.m., March 25) is 10:40 to 11:10 a.m.; 'the next morning' is 5 a.m. to noon; 'the night of' the KITTIWAKE THEFT is 6 p.m. on March 13 to 6 a.m. on March 14; 'the hour before the clocks went forward' is 1 to 2 a.m. PST on March 9 (09:00 to 10:00 UTC); 'after the SURVEILLANCE ended ... until the RECOVERY' is midnight starting March 20 to 10:40 a.m. on March 25.
5. The night of the TRANSFER: 'between 11 p.m. and 4 a.m.' is four real hours and a minute (07:00:00 to 11:00:59.999999 UTC), because 2 to 3 a.m. never came.
6. 'After' an event counts from its last day and 'before' from its first: the day after the TRANSFER (March 8 into 9) is Monday, March 10; two days before the HARBOR THEFT (February 28 into March 1) is Wednesday, February 26, and the Monday after it is March 3; two days after the KITTIWAKE THEFT is March 15; two days before the SURVEILLANCE (March 17 to 19) began is March 15; three days before the RECOVERY is March 22, and the two days after it are March 26 and 27. Relative dates count from the sentence before them: 'the next morning' after March 3 is March 4; 'that night' after the night of the TRANSFER is that night; 'that morning' after March 9 is March 9.
7. Defined terms are read as the affidavit defines them (paragraphs 2 and 3): SKIP = CC-1 = VARLOW, whose phone is Item 1, the GALAXY; CC-2 = COYLE, whose phone is Item 2; DUKE = the HAULER = CC-3 = PELLOQUIN, whose phone is Item 3, the KYOCERA; the SPARE LINE is +13605550163, VARLOW's second number; the WORK NUMBER is +13605550192, PELLOQUIN's work number; the BROKER used +12535550148 and the KELPWISE ACCOUNT, Telegram user id 8820450.
8. A claim is contradicted only when observed data rules it out: the quoted words came from another account, or at another time; a phone holds more messages than an exact count; handles resolve to two accounts; contact is found where the claim says there was none.
9. A curated report cannot show an absence, so an absence claim is never supported here: it is contradicted if contact is found and unproven otherwise. Not found never means did not happen.
10. A meaning or role is supported only when the conversation itself fixes it; an agent's reading of slang, of a nickname or of who directed whom is interpretation.

**Wording**: `plain` claims read like case01's (names, dates and quotes stated
outright). `affidavit` claims use the affidavit's defined terms, aliases, relative or
approximate times, indirect speech, references back to an earlier sentence, roles or
numbers in words.

**Hazard**: the wrong reading the data holds a candidate for. The `wrong` rows below are
the records that reading rests on or misreads; the sheet names only the right person,
account, phone and window.

Hazard kinds:

- `same_name`: a second person with the same first name or nickname
- `second_number`: a second number or account for the same person
- `second_phone`: a second phone that could be meant
- `near_window`: a near-miss window: UTC against local time across midnight, the hour that never came when the clocks went forward, an adjacent day, or Item 3's fixed UTC-8 print
- `contact_name`: one contact name saved on two phones for two people
- `similar_quote`: similar words in another thread
- `none`: no wrong reading planted

as printed in each report: Item 1 prints UTC+0, Item 2 prints device local time with
each row's offset, Item 3 prints every row at a fixed UTC-8. All three phones are set
to America/Los_Angeles (PST, then PDT from 2 a.m. on 2025-03-09, when the clocks went
forward and 2 to 3 a.m. never came). From then on Item 3's printed times are an hour
behind the phone.
