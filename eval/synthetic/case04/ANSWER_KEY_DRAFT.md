Reading rules (how the key reads approximate or defined wording):

1. Times are the phones' own clock, America/Chicago (paragraph 7), never a printed value. Item 1 prints UTC+0. Item 2 prints each row's own offset. Item 3 prints every row at UTC-6, so from 2 a.m. on March 8 (when the clocks went forward) its printed times are an hour behind the phone.
2. 'At about' is 8 minutes either side; 'about four minutes' is 210 to 270 s, 'about one minute' 40 to 80 s, 'about ten minutes' 540 to 660 s, 'a little under three minutes' 150 to 179 s; 'a quarter to midnight' is 11:45 p.m.
3. 'Evening' is 5 p.m. to midnight; 'the early hours' midnight to 6 a.m.; 'the night of' a theft 5 p.m. to 6 a.m.; 'the hours before' the BELMONT SITE theft 6 to 11 p.m.; 'shortly before 5 a.m.' 4:30 to 5 a.m.; 'within fifteen minutes of' 10:30 p.m. is 10:15 to 10:45; 'within half an hour after' it is 10:30 to 11 p.m.
4. 'After the SEARCH' starts the day after it (March 27); 'the rest of March' ends on March 31. 'Monday through Wednesday' before the Thursday SEARCH is March 23 to 25. Relative dates count from the sentence before them: 'the next day' after March 14 is March 15, 'two days later' after March 9 is March 11.
5. Defined terms are read as the affidavit defines them (paragraphs 2 and 3): HAL = SUBJECT-1 = HALLORAN; SUBJECT-2 = FERRO; DEZ = the DRIVER = SUBJECT-3 = OKAFOR; the TARGET PHONE is Item 2, not HALLORAN's Item 1; the PREPAID is +13125550199, HALLORAN's second number; CW-1 is +17735550187; the BUYER is the Instagram account kipflips.chi.
6. A claim is contradicted only when observed data rules it out: the quoted words came from another account, or at another time; a phone holds more messages than an exact count; contact is found where the claim says there was none.
7. A curated report cannot show an absence, so an absence claim is never supported here: it is contradicted if contact is found and unproven otherwise. Not found never means did not happen.
8. A meaning or role is supported only when the conversation itself fixes it; an agent's reading of slang or of who directed whom is interpretation.

**Wording**: `plain` claims read like case01's (names, dates and quotes stated
outright). `affidavit` claims use the affidavit's defined terms, aliases, relative or
approximate times, indirect speech, references back to an earlier sentence, roles or
numbers in words.

**Hazard**: the wrong reading the data holds a candidate for. The `wrong` rows below are
the records that reading rests on or misreads; the sheet names only the right person,
account, phone and window.

Hazard kinds:

- `same_name`: a second person with the same first name or nickname
- `second_number`: a second number or account for the same person
- `second_phone`: a second phone that could be meant
- `near_window`: a near-miss window: UTC against local time across midnight, the DST change, an adjacent day, or Item 3's fixed UTC-6 print
- `contact_name`: one contact name saved on two phones for two people
- `similar_quote`: similar words in another thread
- `none`: no wrong reading planted

Paragraph numbers (¶) are the numbers printed in the affidavit. Times in the tables are
as printed in each report: Item 1 prints UTC+0, Item 2 prints device local time, Item 3
prints every row at a fixed UTC-6. All three phones are set to America/Chicago (CST, then
CDT from 2 a.m. on 2026-03-08).
