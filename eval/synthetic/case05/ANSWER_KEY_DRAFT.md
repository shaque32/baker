Reading rules (how the key reads approximate or defined wording):

1. Times are the phones' own clock, America/Denver (paragraph 7), never a printed value. Item 1 prints each row's own offset. Item 2 prints UTC+0. Item 3 prints every row at UTC-7, so until 2 a.m. on November 2 (when the clocks went back) its printed times are an hour behind the phone.
2. 'At about' is 8 minutes either side; 'about five minutes' is 240 to 360 s, 'about two minutes' 90 to 150 s, 'about eight minutes' 420 to 540 s, 'roughly a minute and a half' 70 to 110 s.
3. 'The early hours' is midnight to 6 a.m.; 'evening' 5 p.m. to midnight; 'morning' 5 a.m. to noon; 'shortly before 6 a.m.' 5:30 to 6 a.m.; 'within an hour after' the SHOP BURGLARY (about 9:40 p.m.) is 9:40 to 10:40 p.m.; 'the hour before' it 8:40 to 9:40 p.m.; 'within half an hour after' the STOP (3:10 p.m.) is 3:10 to 3:40 p.m.
4. The night of the LOCKER BURGLARY: 'the hour after the clocks went back' is the second pass of 1 to 2 a.m., in MST (08:00 to 09:00 UTC); 'between 11 p.m. and 3 a.m.' is five real hours (05:00 to 10:00 UTC), because 1 to 2 a.m. came twice.
5. 'After' an event starts the day after it and 'before' ends the day before: 'the three days after the day of' the SHOP BURGLARY are November 15 to 17, 'two days after' it November 16, 'three days before the STOP' November 16, 'two days after the STOP' November 21, 'the seven days starting the day after the STOP' November 20 to 26, 'from the day after the STOP to the end of November' November 20 to 30. The LOCKER BURGLARY began on Saturday, November 1: 'two days before' it is Thursday, October 30, 'the five days before' it Monday, October 27 to Friday, October 31, 'the Saturday morning before' it November 1, 'the Tuesday after' it November 4. Relative dates count from the sentence before them: 'the next evening' after November 3 is November 4, 'that morning' after November 2 is November 2.
6. Defined terms are read as the affidavit defines them (paragraphs 2 and 3): TOBY = SUBJECT-1 = MERROW, whose phone is Item 1, the SAMSUNG; SUBJECT-2 = VARGA, whose phone is Item 2, the iPHONE; CAL = the COURIER = SUBJECT-3 = ODUM, whose phone is Item 3, the ONEPLUS; the BURNER is +17205550186, MERROW's second number; the FENCE used +17205550129.
7. A claim is contradicted only when observed data rules it out: the quoted words came from another account, or at another time; a phone holds more messages than an exact count; handles resolve to two accounts; contact is found where the claim says there was none.
8. A curated report cannot show an absence, so an absence claim is never supported here: it is contradicted if contact is found and unproven otherwise. Not found never means did not happen.
9. A meaning or role is supported only when the conversation itself fixes it; an agent's reading of slang, of a nickname or of who directed whom is interpretation.

**Wording**: `plain` claims read like case01's (names, dates and quotes stated
outright). `affidavit` claims use the affidavit's defined terms, aliases, relative or
approximate times, indirect speech, references back to an earlier sentence, roles or
numbers in words.

**Hazard**: the wrong reading the data holds a candidate for. The `wrong` rows below are
the records that reading rests on or misreads; the sheet names only the right person,
account, phone and window.

Hazard kinds:

- `same_name`: a second person with the same first name or nickname
- `second_number`: a second number or account for the same person
- `second_phone`: a second phone that could be meant
- `near_window`: a near-miss window: UTC against local time across midnight, the hour that came twice when the clocks went back, an adjacent day, or Item 3's fixed UTC-7 print
- `contact_name`: one contact name saved on two phones for two people
- `similar_quote`: similar words in another thread
- `none`: no wrong reading planted

Paragraph numbers (¶) are the numbers printed in the affidavit. Times in the tables are
as printed in each report: Item 1 prints device local time with each row's offset, Item
2 prints UTC+0, Item 3 prints every row at a fixed UTC-7. All three phones are set to
America/Denver (MDT, then MST from 2 a.m. on 2025-11-02, when the clocks went back and
1 to 2 a.m. came twice). Until then Item 3's printed times are an hour behind the phone.
